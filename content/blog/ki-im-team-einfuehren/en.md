The most honest starting point in many teams looks like this: a few people use ChatGPT every day, a few in secret, the rest not at all. Everyone has their own prompts, nobody shares them, and nobody is quite sure what happens to client data. That isn't an AI strategy – it's **shadow AI.**

The step from "everyone does their own thing" to "the team saves time together" isn't a tool problem. It's a matter of a few clear decisions.

## Start with tasks, not tools

The most common dead end: a team rolls out an AI tool and hopes the value appears on its own. Do it the other way round. Don't ask "What can the tool do?", ask **"Which task annoys us every week?"**

Good first candidates are tasks that

- come up **regularly**,
- consist mostly of **text** (writing, summarising, rephrasing, classifying),
- and aren't a **final decision**, but a draft a human reviews.

Pre-drafting quote text, summarising meeting notes, turning emails into first drafts, searching long documents – these are typical spots where AI saves time right away without anything going wrong.

## Three questions to find your first cases

Sit down with the team once and walk through the week:

1. **Where do we type something similar again and again?** Text snippets, standard replies, recurring emails.
2. **Where do we read a lot to extract a little?** Long emails, PDFs, contracts.
3. **Where do we hate to start because the blank page slows us down?** Drafts, concepts, quotes.

These answers become a short list. It's worth more than any tool demo, because it comes from your real day-to-day.

## Rules, not bans – especially with client data

The question "What are we actually allowed to put in there?" holds many teams back, so settle it early. A few simple rules are enough to start:

- **No personal or confidential client data** in tools where you don't know where the data ends up. When in doubt, anonymise.
- **Review results, don't take them on blindly.** AI phrases things convincingly even when it's wrong.
- **Transparency where it counts:** whatever goes to clients is a human's responsibility.

If you want to do it cleanly, use options that process data in the EU or accounts where your inputs aren't used for training. Which tools suit you depends on what you already work with – from ChatGPT to Claude to Microsoft Copilot.

## Shared templates, not lone wolves

The biggest lever isn't a better tool, it's **shared knowledge.** When one person finds a good prompt, the whole team should be able to use it. Set up a simple collection:

- proven prompts for recurring tasks,
- templates for quotes, emails and summaries,
- short notes on what worked well and what didn't.

That turns ten separate experiments into a shared standard that gets better every week.

## Keep at it

The classic mistake is stopping after the one workshop. AI tools change fast, and the first use cases are rarely the best. Plan short, regular rounds – every few weeks – to collect new cases and share what you've learned.
