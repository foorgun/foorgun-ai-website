Agencies live on people and ideas, yet lose a lot of time on workflows that look the same every week: copying data, building reports, chasing approvals. These are the **seven workflows that most often pay off first at marketing, digital and creative agencies.**

Selection criteria: the workflow comes up **regularly**, has **clear steps** and costs **more time than it should** today.

## 1. Client onboarding

**The problem:** After the contract is signed, someone sets up folders, project, tasks and the CRM record by hand.
**What can run automatically:** folder structure, project template with tasks, CRM record, draft of the welcome email.
**A human reviews:** the welcome email before it's sent.
More on this: [Automating client onboarding](/blog/client-onboarding-automation).

## 2. Monthly client reports

**The problem:** Collecting numbers from several platforms and copying them into a template.
**What can run automatically:** fetching data, preparing it, comparison with the previous month, a draft of the report.
**A human reviews:** the interpretation and the recommendations.
More on this: [Creating client reports automatically](/blog/automated-client-reports).

## 3. Capturing enquiries and following up

**The problem:** Enquiries arrive by form, email and social media. Some get answered too late, and follow-ups get lost.
**What can run automatically:** enquiries land in the CRM in a structured way, a first confirmation goes out, and reminders fire when there's no reply.
**A human reviews:** anything involving a quote or a commitment.

## 4. Quotes and invoices

**The problem:** Quotes are built from old documents, invoices and payment reminders are triggered by hand.
**What can run automatically:** quote draft from the enquiry data, invoicing after a milestone, reminders for open invoices.
**A human reviews:** prices and terms before sending.

## 5. Approvals and feedback loops

**The problem:** A client is supposed to approve drafts, and nobody knows where it's stuck.
**What can run automatically:** approval request with a link, automatic reminder, status overview, a task for the team after the feedback.
**A human reviews:** the actual content of the feedback.

## 6. Moving data between tools

**The problem:** The same information is typed several times into CRM, project tool and billing.
**What can run automatically:** changes in one system are carried over to the others.
**A human reviews:** conflicts, when two systems say different things.
How such connections work technically is explained in [RPA or API?](/blog/rpa-vs-api-automation)

## 7. Research and data upkeep

**The problem:** Lists of contacts, creators or competitors are researched and maintained by hand.
**What can run automatically:** collecting information from public sources, structuring entries, detecting duplicates, an AI summarises.
**A human reviews:** the selection. AI delivers suggestions, not the decision.

## Where to start

Not with all seven. A simple prioritisation:

| Question | If yes |
|---|---|
| Does the workflow come up at least weekly? | good candidate |
| Are the steps always similar? | good candidate |
| Do mistakes or delays happen along the way? | high benefit |
| Are there interfaces to the tools involved? | easier to implement |

Start with the workflow where several of these questions get a "yes", and build **the smallest thing that noticeably helps.** Only then comes the next one.

## What to watch out for

- **Understand the process first**, then automate. Automating an unclear process only makes it unclear faster.
- **Human approval** for anything that goes outside or involves money.
- **Errors must become visible.** A good workflow speaks up when something gets stuck.
- **Keep using your existing tools.** You don't have to replace anything, just connect it. Which platform fits is covered in [n8n or Make?](/blog/n8n-vs-make)
