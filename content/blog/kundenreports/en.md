**Creating client reports automatically** means: the numbers are no longer collected by hand from different platforms and pasted into a template. A workflow fetches them on schedule, prepares them and puts the finished report up for review. You check and comment instead of collecting.

For agencies this is one of the biggest time sinks with the clearest room for improvement: the report is built the same way every month, only the numbers change.

## Why reports take so much time

- The data sits in several platforms (ad accounts, analytics, social media, CRM).
- Every platform exports differently, so the numbers have to be aligned.
- The template is copied and filled in by hand.
- Then comes the most important part, the interpretation. By then there's too little time left for it.

## Two ways: ready-made tool or custom workflow

There are specialised reporting tools for agencies that do exactly this. They're not automatically the worse choice. The honest comparison:

| | Ready-made reporting tool | Custom workflow |
|---|---|---|
| Setup | quick, if your sources are supported | build required |
| Customisation | limited to what the tool can do | report the way your clients know it |
| Data sources | the ones the tool connects | almost anything with an interface, also internal data |
| Running costs | subscription, often per client or account | platform costs, independent of client count |
| Fits when | standard metrics are enough | you need your own formats, sources or commentary |

**Rule of thumb:** if a tool's standard reports are enough for you, use the tool. If you need your own format, several unusual sources or a link to your internal data, a custom workflow pays off.

## How an automated report runs

1. **Schedule:** On the first working day of the month the workflow starts.
2. **Fetch data:** The numbers are pulled from the platforms via their interfaces.
3. **Prepare:** Values are calculated, compared (previous month, target) and put into your format.
4. **Create a draft:** The report is produced as a document, presentation or dashboard link.
5. **Prepare commentary:** An AI can write a first text suggestion on notable changes.
6. **Review:** You or your team read, correct and add the interpretation.
7. **Send:** After approval the report goes to the client.

## The role of AI

AI is good at turning numbers into a **first draft** of explanatory text ("reach went up, cost per click went down"). It doesn't replace your knowledge of the client.

So:

- The text is a **draft**. A human checks before anything goes out.
- The numbers come straight from the sources and aren't "estimated" by the AI.
- Anomalies (drops, outliers) should alert you actively instead of disappearing in a report.

## Typical pitfalls

- **Inconsistent definitions:** What counts as a "lead" or a "conversion"? That has to be settled once.
- **Access expires:** Connections to platforms need monitoring, otherwise numbers suddenly go missing.
- **Too many metrics:** A report with 40 values doesn't get read. Automation is your chance to tighten it.
- **Data protection:** Clarify which data is processed in which tools before connecting client data to new services.

## How to start

1. Take an **existing report** that looks the same every month.
2. Write down where each number comes from.
3. Automate only the **collecting of data** first.
4. Then add comparison, draft and sending after approval.
